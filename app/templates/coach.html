The week is checked for: at most {{ week_rules.cap }} hard runs, {{ week_rules.runs_h }} h apart (RULE-REC-02); {{ week_rules.muscle_h }} h before a muscle group is trained hard again (RULE-REC-01); no hard legs in the {{ week_rules.legs_h }} h before a hard run (RULE-REC-04 — expert opinion, low confidence). Sessions have a day but no time, so the day before counts as within 24 h.

A done session is hard from what you did: a rating of {{ week_rules.srpe_hard }} or more, or {{ week_rules.z3_minutes }}+ minutes above an estimated threshold heart rate (RULE-LOAD-02). Before that, its planned effort decides. Sessions with neither never clash.

A skipped session is dropped rather than made up, and can move only to a free later day that breaks none of these rules (RULE-PROG-01). That is a design choice, not a research finding: no study has compared the options.

This week's running distance (imported runs so far plus planned runs to come) is compared with your last {{ prog.baseline_weeks }} weeks: more injuries were seen when weekly distance rose by more than 20–30 %, though no safe line is proven, and a strict 10 % rule made no difference in a trial (RULE-PROG-02).

After a break you can ease into the rest of the week; how much is your call, since the research gives a range (60–90 %) rather than one number (RULE-PROG-04). Undo puts every changed session back.

Most runs are best kept easy: at 3–4 runs a week about one hard run (RULE-WEEK-01). Counted by sessions here, since planned runs have no heart-rate time yet.
